ChewPaca frequently asked questions

Getting set up

Where should I put the phone?

Propped up, facing your child, roughly at the far side of their plate — about an arm's length away (20–28 in / 50–70 cm). It needs to see their face, so put it somewhere it will not get knocked. A cheap phone stand helps; leaning it against a glass works too. Your child should never hold the phone. If it gets picked up or moved, the app pauses rather than guessing.

It says the camera cannot see a face

Usually one of three things: the phone is too far away, the room is too dim, or your child is looking down at their plate. Move the phone closer, add light, and angle it slightly upward toward their face.

Does it use the microphone?

No. ChewPaca only uses the camera, and never records it.

Running a session

How long should we practice?

One meal a day, at least for the first week. That is not a limitation we added to be annoying — practicing a little and often works considerably better than drilling, and pushing harder tends to turn mealtimes into a struggle.

Nothing happened when my child chewed with their mouth open

That is deliberate. ChewPaca only ever responds to success. When your child keeps chewing with their lips closed, their alpaca or horse chews along and footprints appear, one by one. When they do not, nothing happens at all — no buzzer, no red mark, no tally. Children learn this skill faster from being caught doing it right than from being corrected doing it wrong.

What do I actually do during the meal?

Not much, and that is the point. At the end of each round the app prompts you with one short sentence to say out loud — something specific like "I noticed your lips stayed together while you chewed." That sentence is the part that teaches. The animal holds your child's attention; your praise is what makes the habit stick.

How do I stop a session?

Press and hold the ✕ in the corner for about a second. It takes a deliberate hold so a stray finger cannot end the meal.

The animals

Alpaca or horse?

Either. When you set up a child, choose an alpaca or a horse and pick its look — coat color, and for an alpaca a scarf and backpack, or for a horse an optional white blaze and a saddle and bedroll in colors of your choice. Give it a name, or tap Surprise me.

What happens when a reward is delivered?

Your child's animal walks off to the barn, and they choose a new one — alpaca or horse, any look. Every animal stays in the barn for good. Tap an animal there to watch its walk again; tap its name to rename it.

Can my child use ChewPaca without the animal on screen?

Yes. On the home screen, switch Alpaca (or Horse) off for a plain progress ring with green lines instead. For ages 11–13 this is the default, and either way your child can switch it themselves.

What is the sound at the end of each round?

A soft hum from the alpaca, or a quiet nicker from the horse. It is your cue to say something, because you probably can't see the screen from across the table. Turn it off with the Sound button on the home screen.

Can my child start practice on their own?

Yes. If you set a parent PIN in Settings, your child can still start a session and switch the animal or sound on and off. The PIN is only asked for Settings, bonus tokens, confirming a reward was delivered, adding a child, and practicing beyond the daily limit. Bonus tokens always need a grown-up: without a PIN, the app asks a quick grown-up question instead.

Tokens and rewards

How do tokens work?

A good round earns a token. A few tokens swap for a real-world reward you agreed on beforehand — choosing the bedtime story, picking the music in the car, ten extra minutes before bed. Tokens are never taken away. Once earned, they are earned, even after a bad meal.

Why are the rewards not candy?

Tying food to the lesson tends to turn mealtimes into a negotiation, which is the opposite of what we are going for. Attention, choice, and a small activity work better and cost nothing.

How do stages, rounds and tokens work?

Practice has four stages: Learning, Practicing, Maintaining and Fading out. Every good round earns one token — that never changes. As the habit forms, a reward takes more tokens: 3, then 5, then 8, then 10. Rounds also get longer, from 20 seconds up to 90, depending on your child's age and stage. A new price only applies after the reward your child is currently saving for, and rounds only lengthen once a reward is earned at the new stage, so the goal never moves midway. Moving up a stage takes a minimum number of meals and days plus a steady success rate. Two hard meals in a row pause progress for a few meals; mostly hard recent meals step practice back one stage with shorter rounds. That is normal, not failure. Learning never steps back. In Fading out, log meals where your child chewed well with no app. Two logged app-free meals plus a good run suggest graduating — the aim is for your child not to need the app at all.

Privacy and devices

Does anything leave my phone?

Nothing goes to us — there is no ChewPaca server, no account, and no analytics, and video is never recorded. Your phone's own iCloud or Google backup may include the app's saved progress, like every other app; see the Privacy Policy for how to switch that off.

Moving to a new phone

Because nothing is stored on a server, progress does not sync by itself. In Settings you can export your child's progress to a file and import it on the new device. Restore your unlock in Settings → Purchases.
